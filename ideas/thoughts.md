To extend SepVAE to two diseases, I would keep its original contrastive idea but replace the single salient latent with multiple disease-specific salient blocks. In other words, the encoder would map an X-ray into a shared normal/anatomical latent (c) and two disease latents (s_1) and (s_2), where (c) should explain variation present in both healthy and diseased images, while (s_1) and (s_2) should explain only disease-1 and disease-2 patterns. This is consistent with the original SepVAE design, which already separates a common latent from a salient pathological latent using reconstruction, prior regularization, salient classification, and mutual-information reduction between the spaces. The paper also explicitly points to extending the framework to multiple target datasets or multiple pathology levels, so a multi-disease version is a natural extension rather than a change of principle.  

From a dataset perspective, you would need at least four kinds of supervision: healthy X-rays, disease-1 positive X-rays, disease-2 positive X-rays, and ideally co-morbid cases containing both diseases. Each image should have image-level disease labels, and your bounding boxes should identify the spatial extent of each pathology separately. The healthy set is important because it anchors the common latent (c) to normal anatomy and acquisition variation rather than disease signal, while the diseased sets force the model to use (s_1) and (s_2) when pathology is present. Co-morbid cases are especially valuable because they are the only clean way to test whether the model truly superposes the two disease factors instead of collapsing them into a single mixed pathology code. The boxes are not just extra annotation; they are the main reason the extension is plausible, because they provide a spatial cue for which latent block should explain which abnormal region.

Architecturally, I would keep a ResNet-style encoder backbone because SepVAE is still a VAE at heart and needs strong hierarchical features, but I would add light attention rather than radically redesigning the model. A reasonable addition would be a ResNet encoder with CBAM or a similar channel-and-spatial attention module to improve sensitivity to subtle radiographic patterns, followed by separate heads that predict the posterior parameters of (q(c\mid x)), (q(s_1\mid x)), and (q(s_2\mid x)). If you want to inject the bounding box information during training, cross-attention is a defensible addition, but I would use it sparingly: represent each disease box as a soft spatial mask or learned box token, let intermediate image features attend to the disease-specific box embedding, and use that only as a training-time inductive bias for the disease branches. The decoder can remain shared, but it should reconstruct from ((c,s_1,s_2)), with the option to decode common-only reconstructions by setting (s_1=s_2=0) or to produce disease-specific reconstructions by activating one disease latent at a time. That preserves the original SepVAE logic that full reconstructions contain both common and salient information, while common-only reconstructions reveal what the model believes is healthy structure.  

The loss should start from the SepVAE objective and then split the salient part by disease. At minimum, you want a reconstruction term so the whole latent tuple explains the image, KL terms for (c), (s_1), and (s_2), disease classification losses so (s_1) predicts disease 1 and (s_2) predicts disease 2, and a dependence-reduction term so common and disease latents do not leak into one another. SepVAE already uses an ELBO-style reconstruction-plus-KL objective, a salient classification term, and a mutual-information regularizer between common and salient spaces; here you would generalize that to penalize dependence not only between (c) and each disease latent, but also between (s_1) and (s_2).  A practical form would be
[
\mathcal L=\mathcal L_{\text{rec}}+\beta_c\mathrm{KL}*c+\beta_1\mathrm{KL}*{s_1}+\beta_2\mathrm{KL}*{s_2}
+\lambda_1\mathcal L*{\text{cls},1}+\lambda_2\mathcal L_{\text{cls},2}
+\gamma_{c1}\mathcal L_{\text{dep}}(c,s_1)+\gamma_{c2}\mathcal L_{\text{dep}}(c,s_2)+\gamma_{12}\mathcal L_{\text{dep}}(s_1,s_2).
]
For (\mathcal L_{\text{dep}}), you could use the same MI-style idea as SepVAE or a batch cross-covariance penalty if you want something simpler and more stable.

The bounding boxes let you add the most important new term: a spatial consistency loss. The goal is that the influence of (s_1) should be concentrated inside the disease-1 box and the influence of (s_2) inside the disease-2 box. One way to do this is to decode counterfactual reconstructions using (c) alone, (c+s_1), and (c+s_2), then measure where the reconstruction changes occur. If (|\hat x(c+s_1)-\hat x(c)|) activates strongly outside the disease-1 box, penalize it; similarly for disease 2. If you include cross-attention maps, you can also supervise those maps directly against the bounding boxes with a mask loss such as BCE or Dice. This makes the separation physically grounded: each disease latent is not only predictive of its label, but also responsible for the right region in the image. In practice, that is much stronger evidence of factor separation than orthogonality alone.

Overall, I would frame the method as a multi-salient SepVAE for chest X-rays: the common latent models healthy anatomy and acquisition variability, each disease gets its own salient latent, the disease latents are discouraged from sharing information, and the bounding boxes provide a spatial prior that tells the model where each disease factor is allowed to act. The most conservative version uses a ResNet encoder, optional CBAM for better local feature emphasis, optional cross-attention to box-derived masks during training, a shared decoder, SepVAE’s original reconstruction/KL/classification/MI backbone, and an extra localization loss tied to the annotated boxes. That gives you a slight but meaningful extension of SepVAE without changing its core identity.
